## 250 words

BLOCKWAVE is a free, open-source synthesizer built around a constraint: every sound it makes is a square wave. Two pulse oscillators with independent pulse width and hard sync, a square sub-oscillator, and an NES-style 15-bit LFSR noise generator, anti-aliased with polyBLEP — plus a global RAW switch that turns the anti-aliasing off when the dirt is the point. It covers more ground than it sounds like: pulse-width modulation gives you the movement of detuned oscillators from one voice, hard sync recovers the formants a saw would have provided, and the 128 factory presets run from NES leads to lush pads to sub basses without leaving the square family.

The second idea is how you make patches. Instead of a knob panel, you get a 3×3 bench: a base archetype in the centre, material blocks around it — ICE, LAVA, OBSIDIAN, VOLT and ten more, each applying a defined set of parameter changes. Stack a material for more of it, or pull its individual mix control down if it is too strong. Crafting is deterministic, so the same blocks produce the same sound on every machine and a patch is something you can describe in a sentence. Sixteen exact block patterns are secret recipes that unlock hand-tuned signature sounds, tracked by an in-plugin discovery counter; three are published and thirteen are not.

Underneath is a complete synthesizer: 67 automatable parameters, state-variable filter, two envelopes, two synced LFOs, unison, glide, and a crusher/delay/reverb chain. GPLv3. No account, no upsell.
